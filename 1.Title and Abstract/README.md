Title: ## Predicting Remaining Shelf-Life of Fruits and Vegetables from Daily Images and Storage Conditions: A Longitudinal Study
Expected Abstract

Fruits and vegetables start to spoil after you buy them and how fast they spoil depends on how they look and on how you store them – temperature, humidity and the type of storage. Many studies look at a picture and say the produce is either "fresh" or "rotten." That answer does not tell how many days are left before the produce is no longer usable. Other research tries to estimate shelf life with equipment such as spectral or gas sensors or it uses data from orchards, which makes it hard to use in everyday life. I am proposing to predict the remaining shelf‑life of fruits and vegetables by taking a new picture each day with a smartphone and by recording cheap storage‑condition data. I will build a long‑term dataset of tomatoes, bananas and guavas from markets. Thirty samples of each fruit will be observed daily from the day of purchase until they become unacceptable. Each daily record will contain an image the temperature, the humidity, the storage type (room temperature or refrigerator) and a freshness stage (Fresh, Acceptable, Near‑spoilage, Unacceptable). It will also be labeled with how many days remain. I will compare three methods: one that uses images and lightweight pre‑trained convolutional neural networks such as MobileNetV3, EfficientNet‑B0 or ResNet18; one that uses only environmental data and tree‑based regressors such as Random Forest, XGBoost or LightGBM; and one that combines image features with storage data in a multimodal model. A simple stage‑based average will serve as a baseline. To avoid data leakage all daily records of a sample will stay together in either the training set or the test set and I will evaluate performance with mean absolute error (MAE) root‑mean‑square error (RMSE) and R². I expect the multimodal model to predict remaining shelf‑life accurately, than the single‑modality models and it will do so using only low‑cost equipment. The goal is to help households and small retailers cut food waste and make storage and sale decisions.

Keywords: shelf‑life prediction; remaining life; fruit and vegetable freshness; longitudinal dataset; multimodal learning; transfer learning
